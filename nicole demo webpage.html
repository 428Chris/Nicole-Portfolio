Makerere University gave me my first question: how do you keep power flowing between two solar mini-grids that were never designed to talk to each other? My final-year project answered it with a unified power flow control method — and carried me all the way to the stage of the 11th IEEE PES/IAS PowerAfrica Conference in Johannesburg with a publication in the IEEE Xplore Digital Library.

Along the way I learned systems from the ground up — literally. During industrial training at the Uganda Electricity Transmission Company, I tested and commissioned transmission lines, pole plants and station auxiliaries, and helped re-energize a transformer at Kampala-North substation that had sat dark for eight months. In parallel I've built and run my own business, Nicollection, since 2020, and shipped real software at The Dojo Hub — including the AgriEnergy app, now live on the Play Store.

"Energy systems aren't just engineered — they're contextual. The technology has to fit the place, the people, and the extremes they'll face."

That conviction is what I'm now pursuing full-time as a PhD student in Agricultural and Biological Engineering at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, under Professor Ann-Perry Witmer in the Contextual Engineering Research Group — and as a research intern at the US Army Engineer Research and Development Center (CERL), working on operational energy and the adaptive capacity of critical infrastructure.

2025 → 2026ERDC · Adaptive Capacity

Solar Mini-Grids as Adaptive Capacity

Authoring a paper on solar mini-grids as a resilience measure for critical infrastructure facing increased extreme conditions in Uganda — bound for the Target 4S Conference, June 2026.

Part of the ERDC project "Adaptive Capacity for Risk Analysis of Critical Infrastructure." The paper — "Evaluating the Increasing Importance of Solar Mini-Grids as a Measure of Adaptive Capacity in Response to Potential Increased Extreme Conditions in Uganda" — examines how distributed solar can harden essential services against climate-driven stress.

2023 → 2024Makerere University

Unified Power Flow Controller for Interconnected Mini-Grids

Undergraduate research implementing a power flow control method for two interconnected solar mini-grids — published in the IEEE Xplore Digital Library.

"Implementation of a Unified Power Flow Controller for Two Interconnected Solar Mini-Grids" (2024). Presented at the 11th IEEE PES/IAS PowerAfrica Conference in Johannesburg and at Makerere's Integrated Smart Systems Group annual poster sessions. The work asked how separately built mini-grids can share load reliably once tied together.

Sugarcane Bagasse Drying — Contextual Alignment

Collaborating with the University of Huddersfield, UK, on a comparative analysis of sugarcane bagasse drying technologies under the AFRICOM operational energy effort.

Workstream of the Operational Energy (OE) Africa Command Defense Operational Resilience International Cooperation effort at CERL. The in-progress paper, "Contextual Alignment of Sugarcane Bagasse Drying Technologies: A Comparative Analysis," evaluates which drying approaches actually fit local conditions rather than just performing on paper.
